Cognition and Brain Circuitry Laboratory
Principle Investigator: David Osher, Ph.D.

David Osher

Principle Investigator, Assistant Professor of Psychology
Ph.D., Computational Neuroscience, Massachusetts Institute of Technology

My research explores the relationship between brain connectivity, neural function, and behavior, through computational modeling. Connectivity is the primary constraint on the information available to a brain region, and so connectivity should be highly predictive of neural responses. I am primarily interested in attention and high-level visual perception, and I have demonstrated that structure-function models can predict how your brain will respond during a task, before you even do the task, using your connectivity patterns alone. These models can also predict a variety of cognitive abilities at the level of an individual subject.

Keara Ginell

Graduate Student
B.A., Cognitive Science, Vassar College

I am a third-year graduate student in the Cognitive Neuroscience program advised by Dr. David Osher. After graduating from Vassar College with a bachelor's degree in cognitive science, I spent two years as a clinical research coordinator in the Department of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation at University of Michigan, predominantly working on a project studying cognitive functioning in multiple sclerosis. In the CBC Lab, I study how aspects of cognition (attention, perception, and memory) are shaped by past experiences and learned patterns in the world. I’m especially interested in how the brain encodes and integrates this information, and what happens when these mechanisms are disrupted. My long-term motivation is to inform rehabilitation outcomes for neurological and neuropsychological disorders. Outside the lab, I enjoy running, exploring the outdoors, competing in cycling races, and sipping cappuccinos.

Derrek Montalvo

Graduate Student
B.A., Psychology, Michigan State University

Derrek is a third-year graduate student in Cognitive Neuroscience. He received his B.A. in Psychology from Michigan State University in 2024. While at MSU, he worked with Mark Becker on methods to mitigate the low prevalence effect, improve quitting thresholds, and published a manuscript investigating the impact that a visual search template’s feature information has on search performance and strategy. His research interests include visual attention, perception, and working memory, specifically how visual and spatial information are encoded into working memory and interact to guide and control attention.
